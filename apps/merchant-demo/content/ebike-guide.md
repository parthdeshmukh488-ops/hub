# The honest buyer's guide to e-bikes under €1,500

*By Mara Lindqvist · updated September 2026 · 8 min read*

I have test-ridden more than forty budget e-bikes over the last three years, and most of the advice online is either sponsored or written by people who have never carried a 25 kg bike up to a fourth-floor flat. This guide is the checklist I give friends.

## 1. Start with your route, not the bike

Write down your daily distance, the steepest hill, and whether the bike has to go on a train or up stairs. Those three facts decide more than any spec sheet:

- **Flat, under 15 km each way:** almost any hub-motor bike with a 400–500 Wh battery will do.
- **Real hills:** look for a mid-drive, even if it means stretching the budget.
- **Train or stairs:** a folding bike around 20 kg, and accept the smaller battery.

## 2. The motor: hub or mid-drive

A rear hub motor is quiet and needs almost no maintenance, which is why nearly every bike under €1,300 has one. A mid-drive uses the bike's gears, so it climbs far better, but it wears chains faster. Avoid front hub motors if you ride on wet cobbles: the front wheel can slip when you start uphill.

## 3. Battery: ignore the brochure range

Brochure ranges assume the lowest assist level, a light rider and a warm, flat road. In real mixed riding at the middle level, expect roughly 10–13 km per 100 Wh, and 20–30% less in winter. A removable battery is worth having: you charge it indoors, and it makes the bike less attractive to thieves.

Check two things before you buy: that the maker sells a replacement battery (they cost €350–650), and that the warranty covers the battery for at least two years.

## 4. Brakes are a safety feature, not a spec

With you on it, an e-bike weighs over 100 kg and cruises at 25 km/h. Hydraulic disc brakes stop that mass predictably in the rain. Mechanical discs are acceptable on flat routes; rim brakes are not acceptable on a bike you ride every day.

## 5. Weight and fit

Budget e-bikes weigh 22–28 kg. Lift the bike in the shop, the way you would at home. Then test-ride your frame size for at least ten minutes: a frame that is too big costs you more comfort than any difference in motor torque.

## 6. Service beats a discount

Online-only brands are often €150–300 cheaper, but if the display or controller fails you may wait weeks for a part. Find the nearest workshop that services the bike's motor system before you buy, not after.

## 7. The five-year budget

Plan for the purchase price plus roughly €800–1,200 over five years: one battery, chains and brake pads, a yearly service, and a good lock (€60–120). Theft insurance is worth pricing in cities.

## My shortlist

If I had €1,500 today: the Voltra City 3 for flat commutes, the Kestrel Trail 500 for hills, and the Fjord Fold 20 for train commuters. Whatever you buy, test-ride it first.
